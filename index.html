01 My journey

My Journey: From Finance to Data Analytics Engineering

My work sits where finance, business performance and technology meet. I started in accounting and finance, moved into financial planning and analysis, and followed the questions behind the numbers into data analytics, business intelligence and, eventually, data engineering. Today I combine that business grounding with the engineering needed to make data reliable, so the people who make decisions can trust what they see.

  1. 012014–2022

    Finance and financial operations

    I began as a Financial Analyst, reconciling bank statements and cash books, proofing ledgers and tracking daily collections. As a Payment Analyst I took on daily cash-flow reporting, multi-currency bank reconciliations, payment vouchers and general-ledger postings, and supported the co-insurance, reinsurance and underwriting teams. Eight years of this taught me how money actually moves through a business, and that a number is only useful once it agrees with its source.

  2. 022022–2024

    Financial planning and analysis

    As Lead, Financial Planning & Reporting Analyst I ran the planning cycle for a multi-line insurer: the annual budget with business and regional teams; monthly, quarterly and annual forecasts; budget-versus-actual, variance and expense analysis; scenario modelling; and the CFO and Board packs. I led the automation of budgeting and reporting in Prophix and built a budget template aligned to the IFRS 17 budget model. The role moved me from recording what had happened to helping leadership decide what to do next.

  3. 03From 2020, alongside FP&A

    Data analytics and business intelligence

    Reporting at Board level pushed me towards the data itself. I built and maintained the executive dashboard in Power BI, set up enterprise KPIs, and trained in Power BI, Tableau and data analytics, including a data-analyst track in a 2022 data science fellowship. I have also worked part-time as a BI analyst for a pan-African automotive finance company, tracing wrong dashboard figures to their real cause and fixing them at the right layer.

  4. 04From 2025

    Data engineering

    In January 2025 I moved into data engineering. I build pipelines on Microsoft Fabric and Databricks with PySpark and Delta Lake, design layered Bronze, Silver and Gold data, and use metadata-driven ingestion in Azure Data Factory. That work includes a production pipeline that reconciles digital policy sales to premium, a group-wide view of high-value clients, and an evidence-based general-ledger crosswalk for the move to Dynamics 365 F&O.

  5. 05Today

    Data analytics engineering

    This is where my path was heading. I can sit with finance to agree what counts as a sale or which date sets the week, then build the pipeline, the model and the checks that make the answer hold. My finance background tells me which numbers matter and how they will be challenged. My engineering makes sure they survive the challenge.

03 Approach

How I work

The same six steps on every project, from the first conversation to production. Nothing reaches a report until it passes the checks on the right.

  1. 01

    Agree the definitions

    What counts as a sale, which amount is revenue, which date sets the week.

  2. 02

    Land the raw data

    Source data kept as received, so every figure can be traced back.

  3. 03

    Apply the rules

    Types, dates, channel mapping, duplicates and eligibility in one place.

  4. 04

    Build at a declared grain

    One row per entity before any join, so totals cannot multiply.

  5. 05

    Reconcile

    Counts and amounts checked against the source and finance before use.

  6. 06

    Deploy and explain

    Into production, with results explained in the language finance uses.
