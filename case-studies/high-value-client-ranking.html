High-Value Client Ranking Pipeline

Ranking a multi-line insurance group's most valuable clients in each business, building one group-wide view of every client's value and cross-sell gaps, and catching the join that multiplied their premiums.

01 Overview

Project overview

Ranking pipelines for each business in a multi-line insurance group, and a group single view on top of them, so executive management can see who the most valuable clients are, what they hold and which of the group's products they don't hold yet.

02 Context

Business context

A multi-line group serves the same clients through several businesses, each with its own records. Executive management wanted a clear answer to a simple question: who are our most valuable clients?

03 Problem

Problem statement

Answering it reliably raised several problems:

  • Each business ranked its own top clients separately. Leadership could not see one client's total value across general insurance, life, health, asset management and pensions, or which products that client did not hold yet.
  • Different measures of value: premium for insurers, assets for the asset manager, plus tenure.
  • Name-based rules were classifying some clients as individuals or corporates incorrectly.
  • A one-to-many join to portfolio data multiplied premium totals, exactly at the top of the list where executives look first.

04 Objectives

Objectives

  1. Build a client-ranking pipeline for each business.
  2. Classify clients correctly as individuals or corporates.
  3. Calculate each client's value at the correct grain: one row per client.
  4. Rank clients on premium, assets and tenure.
  5. Link clients across businesses with a shared identifier for a group-wide view.
  6. Specify and prototype an executive Power BI report on the ranked output.

05 My role

My role and contribution

I built this for top executive management.

  • Built the ranking pipelines for each business in PySpark, covering over a thousand clients.
  • Found and fixed the join fan-out, and added the checks that now catch it.
  • Corrected the client-type classification rules.
  • Designed the Gold-layer output and helped create the cross-business customer table.
  • Built the group single view on top of the rankings (in build).
  • Wrote the Power BI build specification and built an interactive HTML prototype.

06 Architecture

Solution architecture

Business source data → ingestion → cleaning and client-type classification → client-level aggregation (fan-out control) → shared client identifier → ranking on premium, assets and tenure → Gold output → group single view and executive reporting

08 Implementation

Implementation approach

Rankings for each business

  • General and life insurance: premium from general-ledger entries on gross-premium and inward-reinsurance accounts, linked to policy and customer, with claims paid set against it.
  • Health: family-level premium and covered lives, grouping members on the enrollee ID and falling back to a primary member where none is flagged.
  • Asset management: inflow, outflow, net value and turnover per client for FY2025 and H1 2026, ranked with tie-breakers.

The fan-out fix

The portfolio data holds more than one row per client. Joining it directly to client-level premium repeated each client's premium for every match, so totals came out many times too high.

  • Portfolio data is now brought to one row per client before the join.
  • Row counts and premium totals are compared before and after each join, so any future fan-out shows up at once.

Group single view (in build)

  • Keys every client on a phone-based identity ID, with each business's join key chosen by match rate.
  • Writes three tables: a summary with one row per client (businesses held, value in each, group rank and cross-sell gaps), a detail table for drill-through, and an unlinked table for data-quality follow-up.
  • Includes a lookup helper that finds any client by phone number.

09 Data quality

Data quality and validation

  • Grain check: one row per client before ranking.
  • Join cardinality check: row counts compared before and after each join.
  • Premium reconciliation: totals after joining equal totals before joining.
  • Classification check against known individual and corporate clients.
  • Duplicate enrollee rows removed before summing health premium.
  • Policy lookup deduplicated before the group join, so general-ledger lines and claims do not multiply.
  • Key and amount fields cast to their expected types before filtering and joining.

10 Results

Results and business impact

One CEO-level view of the group's top clients, with each client's cross-sell gaps and a list of high-value records that can't be linked yet.

  • A ranked view of the group's most valuable clients, on correct client-level figures.
  • A fair ranking combining insurance premium, investment assets and tenure.
  • Correct individual and corporate segments.
  • Protection against inflated figures reaching executive reports.

Measured impact: not yet quantified. No revenue, time-saving or accuracy figures are claimed. Company names, real figures and internal rules are withheld.

Still to do: finish the group single view, and build the executive Power BI report from the approved specification.

11 Lessons

Key lessons and capabilities

A pipeline can run without errors and still produce figures many times too large. Only reconciling totals before and after each join exposes it.

What this project taught me

  • Join fan-out: bring data to client grain before joining, and reconcile totals before and after.
  • Name-based classification: corrected rules and a review against known clients fixed misclassified segments.
  • Different value measures: premium and assets had to be combined deliberately, not added together.
  • Client identity across businesses: a shared identifier was needed for a group-wide view.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • PySpark pipeline design
  • Grain and join control
  • Customer data modelling
  • Executive reporting specification
