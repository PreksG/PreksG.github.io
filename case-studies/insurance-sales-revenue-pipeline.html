01 Overview

Project overview

A production pipeline on Microsoft Fabric and Databricks that brings digital policy sales for Life and General Business, and their premium, into one reconciled, reporting-ready dataset for weekly sales and revenue analysis.

02 Context

Business context

Digital distribution channels, including the intermediary web portal used by brokers and agents, create policy sales across different systems and records. Operations, sales and finance need to work from the same numbers, and sales figures are only useful if they agree with what finance recognises.

03 Problem

Problem statement

Sales and premium information was not brought together consistently:

  • No single view of digital policy purchases for Life and General Business.
  • Week-to-week monitoring was inconsistent because extracts used different dates and rules.
  • Sales records were hard to reconcile with the related premium.
  • Channel values were recorded under different codes and spellings.
  • Duplicated, ineligible or stale records could reach reports unnoticed.
  • Portal sales by brokers and agents could not be tied to recognised revenue, because the portal's data sat in separate databases for general and life business.

04 Objectives

Objectives

  1. Consolidate digital policy sales for Life and General Business into one analytical structure.
  2. Establish consistent transformation and business rules.
  3. Associate each policy purchase with the correct premium.
  4. Reconcile counts and premium before the data is used.
  5. Enable weekly analysis of purchases and revenue, including channel comparison.
  6. Deliver the pipeline to production as a repeatable foundation for reporting.

05 My role

My role and contribution

I led delivery. A data analyst built the transformation and business-rule logic under my supervision, and a senior business stakeholder sponsored the project.

  • Understood the business requirement for digital sales and premium reporting.
  • Landed the source data in the raw (Bronze) layer.
  • Agreed the business rules and kept the end-to-end process on track.
  • Supervised and reviewed the transformation work that produced the Gold dataset.
  • Pushed the pipeline to production on Microsoft Fabric and Databricks.

06 Architecture

Solution architecture

Sources and intermediary portal → ingestion (Azure Data Factory) → Bronze (raw, as received) → transformation and business rules → Gold (reporting-ready) → reconciliation of counts and premium → weekly sales and revenue analysis

08 Implementation

Implementation approach

Definitions agreed before building

The approved answers are internal, so they are listed here as questions:

  • Which record represents a completed digital policy purchase?
  • Is the analytical record at policy, transaction or payment level?
  • Which authoritative record supplies the premium used in reporting?
  • Which date places a sale in a week, and from which date is data in scope?
  • Which control totals validate the output?

Business rules

Applied in the step that builds the Gold dataset:

  • Data-type standardisation and date normalisation.
  • Channel classification against an agreed channel list.
  • A reporting start date, a positive-premium filter, deduplication on an agreed business key, and a latest-transaction-date check.
  • An explicit grain, so joining policy activity to premium cannot inflate amounts.

Intermediary portal ingestion (Azure Data Factory)

Two pipelines, one per business line, bring in sales made by brokers and agents:

  • General business: a Lookup finds the policy-transactions table in the portal's product-mapping database, and a sequential ForEach copies it to ADLS Gen2. Three Databricks notebooks then land it, promote it to Silver and build the Gold web revenue-realisation output.
  • Life business: the table list (products and proposal details) is held as a pipeline variable that maps each source table to its folder. For each table the pipeline deletes the existing file and then copies to Parquet, so reruns never duplicate data.

09 Data quality

Data quality and validation

  • Fields cast to their expected types; business dates parsed and in period.
  • Only transactions from the agreed start date, and only positive premium.
  • Duplicates removed on the agreed business key; channels mapped to the agreed list.
  • Latest transaction date checked, so stale loads are caught.
  • Joins made at the declared grain to prevent row multiplication.
  • Record counts and premium totals reconciled before the data is used.

10 Results

Results and business impact

  • One consistent, reporting-ready view of digital policy purchases and premium for Life and General Business.
  • A single agreed definition of a sale and of premium, instead of conflicting extracts.
  • Reconciliation built into the pipeline rather than done by hand afterwards.
  • Earlier detection of duplicate, ineligible and stale records.
  • Portal sales for both business lines flowing into a Gold revenue-realisation view, with safe reruns.
  • A production foundation that other reports can build on.

Measured impact: not yet quantified. No revenue, time-saving or accuracy figures are claimed. Company names, real figures and internal rules are withheld.

Possible next steps, not yet built: a reusable Silver layer for the digital-sales business rules, automated reconciliation alerts, a published KPI dictionary, and a synthetic public demonstration.

11 Lessons

Key lessons and capabilities

A technically successful pipeline is not evidence that reported revenue is correct. The output has to agree with an authoritative control total, using consistent dates, rules and definitions.

What this project taught me

  • Different representations of a sale across sources: agreeing the sales event and grain first avoided mismatched totals.
  • Purchase date versus payment date: the date that places a sale in a week had to be agreed before weekly figures could reconcile.
  • Silent type errors: in a related dashboard, text-versus-integer mismatches produced wrong weekly results without any error, so types are now enforced early.
  • Coordinating delivery: making data available on time and deploying safely was as much of the job as the transformations.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • Delivery leadership
  • Lakehouse design
  • Reconciliation controls
  • Production deployment
  • Finance-aligned definitions
