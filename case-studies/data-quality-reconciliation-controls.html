01 Overview

Project overview

Not a single project but the practice behind all of them: the controls I build into each stage of a pipeline or report, collected from the case studies on this site.

02 Context

Business context

My finance background taught me that a number is only useful once it agrees with its source. The same rule applies to data: a pipeline can run without errors and still produce figures many times too large.

03 Problem

Problem statement

Across these projects the same failure modes kept appearing:

  • A one-to-many join that multiplied premiums at the top of a client ranking (High-Value Client Ranking).
  • Duplicated, ineligible or stale records that could reach reports unnoticed (Sales-to-Revenue).
  • Text-versus-integer mismatches that produced wrong weekly results without any error (a dashboard related to Sales-to-Revenue).
  • Truncated history, duplicate loads and a filter conflict from an earlier fix (Dashboard Root-Cause Analysis).
  • Reports at risk of going out on stale data, and measures that looked mis-scaled (AI Weekly Report).
  • An incomplete official mapping file during a ledger migration (GL Crosswalk).

04 Architecture

Solution architecture

Source → landing (counts, freshness) → rules (types, dates, eligibility, duplicates) → model (declared grain, join cardinality) → output (reconcile to control totals) → publication (freshness gate)

05 Implementation

Implementation approach

At the source and landing

  • Latest-load and freshness checks, so stale loads are caught (Sales-to-Revenue; Production vs Budget).
  • Tables discovered at run time and delete-before-copy, so nothing is missed and reruns never duplicate (Lakehouse architecture).

In the business rules

  • Types cast before filtering and joining; dates parsed and checked against the reporting period.
  • Eligibility rules such as a reporting start date and positive premium only.
  • Duplicates removed on an agreed business key; channels mapped to an agreed list.

In the model

  • A declared grain: one row per entity before any join.
  • Row counts and totals compared before and after every join, so fan-out shows up at once.
  • Distinct-count and history checks on tables feeding dashboards.

At the output

  • Record counts and premium totals reconciled to source and finance control totals.
  • Actuals agreed to the general ledger before variance analysis; business-unit budgets add up to the group plan.
  • Parallel-run transactions matched 1:1 and monthly totals compared per account.

Before publication

  • A freshness gate: no AI briefing is sent unless every model has refreshed.
  • Unvalidated measures labelled "Monitored" rather than reported as fact.
  • Headline dashboard figures compared with an independent source.

06 Results

Results and business impact

Wrong, inflated and stale figures caught before they reach executive reports and decisions.

  • Protection against inflated figures reaching executive reports (High-Value Client Ranking).
  • Reconciliation built into pipelines rather than done by hand afterwards (Sales-to-Revenue).
  • Wrong figures found and explained before they drove decisions (Dashboard Root-Cause Analysis).
  • Protection against decisions based on stale data (AI Weekly Report).

Measured impact: not yet quantified. No revenue, time-saving or accuracy figures are claimed. Company names, real figures and internal rules are withheld.

07 Lessons

Key lessons and capabilities

A technically successful pipeline is not evidence that the numbers are right. Only reconciling totals before and after each step proves it.

What this project taught me

  • The symptom is rarely where the cause is: most causes sit upstream of the report.
  • Interim fixes create new problems, so measures are retested after every fix.
  • Distinct counts are not always distinct: check what one row represents before counting.

Capabilities demonstrated
