01 Overview

Project overview

For nearly three years I led financial planning and reporting for a multi-line insurance group: the annual budget, rolling forecasts, variance and scenario analysis, and the CFO and Board packs, plus the executive dashboard and the automation of budgeting in Prophix.

02 Context

Business context

An insurance group plans across several businesses, departments and regions, each with its own drivers and assumptions. Leadership needs one consistent plan, an early view of where results are heading, and a clear explanation of why actual performance differs from plan.

03 Problem

Problem statement

Without a disciplined cycle, common problems appear:

  • Budgets built in disconnected spreadsheets that are hard to consolidate.
  • Forecasts updated late or inconsistently across business units.
  • Variances reported without a clear explanation of what drove them.
  • Planning that does not line up with the accounting basis, which became critical with IFRS 17.
  • Manual, repetitive preparation of reporting packs every month.

04 Objectives

Objectives

  1. Deliver the annual budget, aligned with corporate goals, with business, departmental and regional stakeholders.
  2. Maintain monthly, quarterly and annual forecasts.
  3. Explain variances between budget, forecast and actual results.
  4. Model scenarios to support strategic decisions.
  5. Produce CFO and Board packs with clear recommendations.
  6. Track enterprise KPIs in an executive dashboard.
  7. Automate budgeting and reporting workflows, and keep budgeting consistent with the IFRS 17 budget model.

05 My role

My role and contribution

A recurring responsibility rather than a one-off build, as Lead, Financial Planning & Reporting Analyst. Before this role I spent eight years as a financial analyst and payment analyst.

  • Led the annual budget cycle with business, departmental and regional stakeholders.
  • Produced monthly, quarterly and annual forecasts, financial models and scenario analyses.
  • Prepared CFO and Board reporting packs with variance analysis and recommendations.
  • Built and maintained the CEO and executive dashboard in Power BI.
  • Led the automation of budgeting and reporting workflows in Prophix, and built the IFRS 17-aligned budget template.
  • Carried out expense analysis, general-ledger reviews and peer benchmarking.

06 Architecture

Solution architecture

Business-unit inputs → budget template (IFRS 17-aligned) → consolidation in Prophix → rolling forecasts → actuals from the general ledger → variance and scenario analysis → CFO and Board packs and executive dashboard

08 Implementation

Implementation approach

  • Budget preparation: every unit planned on the same IFRS 17-aligned template.
  • Consolidation and automation: manual consolidation steps replaced with a structured planning process in Prophix.
  • Forecasting: updated monthly, quarterly and annually, giving leadership an early view of results against budget.
  • Variance and scenario analysis: actuals from the general ledger compared with budget and forecast, variances explained by their drivers.
  • Executive reporting: CFO and Board packs with commentary and recommendations, and the executive dashboard for ongoing KPI tracking.

09 Data quality

Data quality and validation

  • Ledger reconciliation: actuals agree with the general ledger before analysis.
  • Consolidation check: business-unit budgets add up to the group plan.
  • Template consistency: every unit plans on the same IFRS 17-aligned template.
  • Variance completeness: every material variance has an explanation.
  • Expense and ledger review: unusual postings and trends investigated.
  • Version control: each forecast round kept separately.

10 Results

Results and business impact

  • One consistent group budget aligned with corporate goals.
  • Regular forecasts that gave leadership an early view of results.
  • Clear, explained variances in CFO and Board reporting.
  • Scenario analysis to support strategic decisions.
  • Less manual effort through Prophix automation, with budgeting kept consistent with IFRS 17.

Measured impact: not yet quantified. No revenue, time-saving or accuracy figures are claimed. Company names, real figures and internal rules are withheld.

11 Lessons

Key lessons and capabilities

Years of reconciling, explaining and defending numbers to a CFO and a Board taught me what decision-makers need from data, and why figures must be right before they are presented.

What this project taught me

  • Many contributors, one plan: consistent inputs needed a common template and a clear timetable.
  • A new accounting standard: budgets had to be aligned to the IFRS 17 budget model.
  • Explaining, not just reporting: Boards want to know why results moved, so variance analysis had to identify drivers.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • Budgeting and forecasting
  • Variance and scenario analysis
  • Board reporting
  • Prophix automation
  • IFRS 17 budget modelling
