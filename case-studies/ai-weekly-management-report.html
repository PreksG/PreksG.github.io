AI-Assisted Weekly Management Intelligence Report

01 Overview

Project overview

Every Monday, after the Power BI models refresh, an AI assistant queries six semantic models, compares the current position with the prior month and drafts a structured leadership briefing. If any model has not refreshed, the report is held and I receive a "not sent" alert instead.

02 Context

Business context

Senior leadership in an automotive lending business relied on six separate Power BI reports covering credit, portfolio performance, collections, collections operations, credit decisioning and an executive view. Each answered its own question; none said what mattered across the business this week.

03 Problem

Problem statement

Leadership had the data but not the synthesis:

  • Six reports had to be opened and read separately.
  • Connections across areas, such as credit decisions feeding collections risk, were not visible in any single report.
  • Reports described the past; leadership wanted a forward view and recommended actions.
  • Preparing a cross-business summary by hand every week was time-consuming.
  • Some measures were not yet validated, so a summary could repeat a wrong number with confidence.

04 Objectives

Objectives

  1. Inventory the six semantic models and document what each measures.
  2. Build a KPI dictionary and map every KPI to its source model.
  3. Design a briefing structure that leadership can read in minutes.
  4. Automate the weekly analysis and drafting with an AI assistant.
  5. Prevent the report from going out on stale or failed data.
  6. Make every figure traceable to its source report.

05 My role

My role and contribution

I designed and built the whole workflow.

  • Inventoried the six semantic models and documented what each measures.
  • Built the KPI dictionary, the KPI-to-source mapping and the cross-model map.
  • Wrote the analysis framework and data-quality notes the AI works from.
  • Set up the AI assistant's access to the models and the weekly schedule.
  • Designed the freshness gate, the failure alert and the "Monitored" treatment for unvalidated measures.

06 Architecture

Solution architecture

Six semantic models → model inventory and KPI dictionary → freshness gate → AI analysis across models → structured weekly briefing → HTML email to leadership

08 Implementation

Implementation approach

  • Foundation first: each model documented, every KPI mapped to its source, and a cross-model map of how areas affect one another.
  • Freshness gate: the Monday run first checks that every model has refreshed and can be reached.
  • Analysis: the assistant queries the models directly, compares with the prior month and reads across areas using the cross-model map.
  • Fixed structure: executive summary, management scorecard, KPI detail by area, emerging risks, opportunities, recommended actions, a short CEO brief and a sources list.

09 Data quality

Data quality and validation

  • Freshness gate: no report is sent unless every model has refreshed and is reachable.
  • Single source per KPI, as named in the KPI dictionary.
  • Suspected mis-scaled measures labelled "Monitored", not reported as fact.
  • Every report lists its sources.
  • Figures always compared with the prior month.

10 Results

Results and business impact

  • One weekly view across six reports, instead of six separate reads.
  • A forward-looking briefing with risks, opportunities and recommended actions.
  • Cross-business connections that no single report showed.
  • Protection against decisions based on stale data.
  • A reusable foundation (inventory, KPI dictionary, cross-model map) for future reporting.

Measured impact: not yet quantified. No revenue, time-saving or accuracy figures are claimed. Company names, real figures and internal rules are withheld.

11 Lessons

Key lessons and capabilities

An AI summary is only as trustworthy as the data and rules underneath it. The gate and the KPI dictionary matter more than the writing.

What this project taught me

  • AI can sound confident about wrong numbers: the KPI dictionary, single-source rule and "Monitored" label keep the briefing honest.
  • Stale data: the freshness gate stops the report going out early.
  • Reading across reports: connecting six reports needed a documented map of how the areas relate.

Capabilities demonstrated

  • AI-assisted reporting
  • Semantic-model documentation
  • KPI governance
  • Workflow automation
